# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

High school students studying math, mostly at an international school in Seoul, looking for help with a specific course or topic: what a course is like before they take it, how to approach a problem type they struggle with, or a short clear explanation of one topic. A second group is student contributors (minors) who write those resources and want to know exactly what to send and how.

## Product Purpose

Math Mentors is a math resource-sharing hub written by students, for students. Students share Course Guides, Problem Spotlights, Short Notes and Guides. Every post is short and is checked once by an editor before it goes up. Success means students find a useful post for their course, and more students contribute.

## Positioning

First-hand experience from students who just took a course, and short, clear, student-written resources. Khan Academy, AoPS and Reddit do not offer reviewed, course-specific advice from peers at this level.

## Operating Context

- Student-run volunteer organization. Website math-mentors.org, contact contact@math-mentors.org.
- Submissions arrive by email only. Editors review them and add each post by hand as one Markdown file (plus a PDF if there is one). No accounts, forms or uploads.
- Growth ladder: Stage 1 the hub (now); Stage 2 longer expository pieces (3–5 pages, full proofs); Stage 3 a student math journal, Vol. 1. The site should leave room for a future Journal section but not build it.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Content types: Course Guide (600–1200 words), Problem Spotlight (1–2 pages), Short Note (1–3 pages, PDF, LaTeX preferred), Guide (longer full-topic PDF).
- Post metadata: title, type, course or topic tag, level (intro / intermediate / advanced), contributor display name and grade, date published, optional AI-use statement (no reviewer shown on posts).
- Rules: one-editor review; no copyrighted scans; no answers to current graded work; contributors shown by first name plus grade or username only; AI use allowed for LaTeX help and checking, disclosed.
- Analytics: Vercel Web Analytics (page views). Post and contributor counts computed from post files.
- Open decisions: final name and tagline (keep "Math Mentors"), which courses get featured first, who reviews posts, team and faculty advisor names. Do not name the school on the site.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Math Mentors. Logo mark at public/mark.png.
- Copy follows the user's website-rules skill: plain technical voice, headings that name things, no slogans.

## Evidence on Hand

- One published post: A Guide to Complex Numbers by Juho Lee (founder, Grade 10), content/posts/a-guide-to-complex-numbers.md.
- Founder: Juho Lee (shown by full name). No testimonials, stats or other team names. Do not invent any.

## Product Principles

- Concrete over promotional: every page explains what happens and what to send.
- Adding a post must never need code changes.
- Protect contributors: they are minors, so show minimal identity.
- Start small and leave room to grow toward the journal.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Many readers are non-native English speakers at an international school, so use plain wording and clear structure. Math must stay readable (KaTeX, adequate size and contrast).
