A 33-page guide that starts from the definition of $i$ and ends with Euler's formula, $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$.

It assumes algebra (linear and quadratic equations, radicals, exponents), basic trigonometry and the unit circle. Polar coordinates help but are not required.

1. Introduction to complex numbers and the complex plane
2. Characteristics of complex numbers
3. The polar form and Euler's formula

Each section ends with a problem set, followed by full solutions.
